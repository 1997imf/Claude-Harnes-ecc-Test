See why the slope of x² at x = 2 is 4, by watching secant lines turn into the tangent line.

• Here is the curve f(x) = x².
• We want the steepness of the curve exactly at the point P = (2, 4).
• Pick a second point Q, a distance h to the right, and draw the line through P and Q.
• The slope of this secant line works out to Slope = 4 + h.
• Now slide Q toward P by shrinking h.
• With h = 0.01 the secant slope is already 4.01.
• In the limit, the secant lines become the tangent line.
• So the derivative at x = 2 is 4: the slope of the tangent line.

Result: f′(2) = 4 for f(x) = x².
